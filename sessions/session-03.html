Session 3: Components of RAG

Breaking Down the Building Blocks

The Three Core Components

Every RAG system has three essential components that work together to retrieve information and generate responses. Understanding these components is crucial to understanding how RAG works.

1. Document Store / Knowledge Base

The document store is where all your information lives. It's the foundation of your RAG system. Think of it as a library, database, or collection of resources that your system can search through.

What Goes in a Document Store?

  • Company documents and policies
  • Product manuals and documentation
  • Research papers and articles
  • FAQs and knowledge base articles
  • Web pages and blog posts
  • Internal reports and memos
  • Scientific papers
Important: The quality of your document store directly affects the quality of RAG responses. If your documents are outdated, inaccurate, or incomplete, your RAG system will produce poor results.

Document Store Characteristics

  • Structured or Unstructured: Can store various formats (PDF, HTML, plain text)
  • Searchable: Must allow efficient searching and retrieval
  • Scalable: Should handle growing amounts of data
  • Updatable: Easy to add, remove, or modify documents

2. Retriever

The retriever is the system's search engine. When a user asks a question, the retriever's job is to find the most relevant documents or passages from the document store that could help answer that question.

How Retrievers Work

Analogy: Imagine a librarian with superpowers

Traditional librarian: "Give me a question, and I'll search the card catalog"
Retriever: "Give me a question, and I'll understand its meaning and find the most relevant books instantly"

Types of Retrieval Methods

  • Keyword-based: Searches for exact words (like Google search)
  • Semantic: Understands meaning, finds similar concepts
  • Vector-based: Uses embeddings to find semantically similar content
  • Hybrid: Combines multiple retrieval methods

Retriever Metrics

  • Relevance: Does it find the right documents?
  • Speed: How quickly does it retrieve results?
  • Ranking: Does it rank most relevant results first?

3. Generator (Language Model)

The generator is the language model that reads the retrieved documents and generates a natural, coherent response to the user's question. It's like a writer that has all the facts it needs.

Generator's Role

  • Reads retrieved documents
  • Understands the user's question
  • Synthesizes information from multiple sources
  • Writes a clear, natural response
  • Potentially cites sources
Key Difference from Traditional LLMs: The generator doesn't need to have all the world's knowledge in its parameters. It just needs to be good at reading and writing. This allows for smaller, faster models!

Generator Characteristics

  • Instruction Following: Follows prompts well
  • Context Understanding: Understands provided documents
  • Quality Writing: Produces clear, coherent responses
  • Efficiency: Can be smaller than traditional LLMs

How They Work Together

Scenario: Customer asks about warranty

1. User Question: "What's the warranty on your laptops?"

2. Retriever: Searches the document store and finds warranty policy, product specs, and FAQ

3. Generator: Reads the retrieved documents and writes: "Our laptops come with a 2-year hardware warranty covering manufacturing defects..."

4. Response: Accurate, sourced answer delivered to user

Component Dependencies

The three components depend on each other:

  • Document Store Quality: Affects both retriever and generator
  • Retriever Quality: Directly affects what generator can write about
  • Generator Quality: Affects how well the retrieved information is communicated

Optional Components

Some advanced RAG systems add additional components:

  • Query Optimizer: Improves how questions are understood
  • Re-ranker: Re-ranks retrieved documents for better relevance
  • Filter/Router: Decides which component to use
  • Cache: Stores frequently asked questions

Key Takeaways

  • RAG consists of three core components: Document Store, Retriever, and Generator
  • Document Store quality is critical - it's the foundation of RAG
  • Retriever finds relevant information based on the question
  • Generator writes responses based on retrieved information
  • All three components work together for effective RAG
  • Generator can be smaller and more efficient than traditional LLMs
